Broome Business Support: Capability & Tendering.

Plenty of businesses up here can do the work but never make the shortlist. Usually that's not about the work. It's about what the buyer could see on paper.

The gap between being good and looking good on paper

You've got the plant, the crew, the safety record and the years. What you might not have is the paperwork buyers use to judge you — a proper capability statement, the right registrations, a written safety system, and tender answers in the format they're marking against.

So the submission goes in and it doesn't get up. Not because you couldn't do the job. Because the person scoring it couldn't tell that you could.

What we do here: get your business documented, get you registered in the right places, and help you answer specific tenders. It's practical work with something at the end of it, not business coaching.

What you get

Capability statement

A document written the way government and big contractors read them — not a brochure.

  • What you do, in their words
  • Jobs you've done (real ones, with permission)
  • Your key people and their tickets
  • Your safety record and system
  • Licences, certificates and registrations
  • ABN, business structure, insurances

Tender response help

For a specific job you want to go after.

  • Reading the tender and pulling out what's actually required
  • Structuring the answers against the criteria
  • Pricing support and how to present it
  • Teaming up with another business where that helps
  • Going through the debrief afterwards, win or lose

Getting registered in the right places

Government buying has its own systems, and if you're not in them you don't get asked. We'll get you set up and tell you which ones actually matter for the work you do.

  • WA Government supplier registration
  • Aboriginal business registers, where they apply to you
  • Resources company supplier portals and pre-qualification panels
  • Local government and shire supplier lists

How the local content rules affect you

WA Government contracts and big resources jobs have rules requiring the head contractor to use local businesses, and Aboriginal-owned businesses in particular. That's a real opportunity — but only for businesses a buyer can find, check and show on their reporting.

Being eligible under those rules and winning work under them are two different things. The gap is mostly documentation. That's fixable.

If you're an Aboriginal-owned business

  • The Aboriginal Procurement Policy creates genuine opportunity, and head contractors are actively looking
  • What they need to see is capability they can verify and capacity they can rely on
  • We know the Kimberley Aboriginal business landscape directly — who's operating, what they can do, and what's actually getting in the way
  • The support is built around your business, not a template

What it costs

Fixed fee per piece of work. A capability statement has a set price and a set deliverable. Tender help is quoted per tender. If you've got work coming up regularly, an ongoing arrangement usually works out better.

We don't publish fees because the right number depends on what you need. Ask and you'll get a figure before you commit.

Something you should know

Nausicaa also works for contractors from outside the region — sometimes the same ones you'd be tendering to. We tell you that up front rather than letting you find out.

The firm rules: we won't work for two businesses on opposite sides of the same job. We won't use anything you tell us to help someone else, now or later. Where we're running the contract for a head contractor, we won't also be coaching businesses bidding for work under it. We check before taking anything on, and we write that check down. Sometimes we say no.
