The gap between being capable and looking capable

Many businesses in the Kimberley have the operational capability to perform government and resources contracts. They have the plant, the people, the safety records and the experience. What they often lack is the documentation — capability statements, pre-qualification submissions, WHS management systems, financial reporting in the format buyers expect — that allows procurement teams to assess them properly.

The result is that capable businesses do not make shortlists. Not because they cannot perform the work, but because their submission did not communicate that capability in the language buyers use to evaluate it.

This service addresses that gap. It is not generic business development coaching — it is specific, practical support to produce the documentation and responses that move a business from invisible to shortlisted.

What this service covers

Capability statements

A capability statement written for government procurement, not for a general audience. Format, content and language aligned to how buyers actually evaluate suppliers.

  • Trade and service scope description
  • Relevant project experience (verified, with consent)
  • Key personnel and qualifications
  • Safety record and WHS system summary
  • Certifications, licences and registrations
  • ABN, business structure, insurance summary

Tender response support

For specific tenders — government RFT, resources RFQ, or head contractor invitation — where structured response support improves the quality of the submission.

  • Tender document review and requirement mapping
  • Response structure and selection criteria answering
  • Pricing support and commercial structure
  • Subcontracting and teaming arrangements
  • Debrief review after shortlisting decisions

Government procurement navigation

WA Government procurement has its own language, its own supplier registration requirements (Supplier Registration System, Disability Employment Register, Registered Training Organisation lists) and its own evaluation frameworks. A business that has not previously engaged with government procurement needs a working understanding of how it operates before a tender is worth submitting.

This includes: how the WAIPS and APP frameworks affect which businesses get considered for subcontract packages, how to position on the Kimberley Supplier Register, what the Social Procurement Framework asks buyers to consider, and when a teaming or joint venture arrangement improves the likelihood of success over a solo bid.

Aboriginal business focus

The Aboriginal Procurement Policy creates genuine commercial opportunity for Aboriginal businesses in the Kimberley — but only for businesses that are documented, registered and able to demonstrate capability to a government buyer. The gap between a business that qualifies under APP and one that wins work under it is largely a documentation and presentation gap.

Nausicaa Consulting has direct working knowledge of the Kimberley Aboriginal business landscape — which businesses are operating, what capability they hold, and what the structural barriers are to their accessing procurement opportunities. That context shapes the support, rather than applying a generic capability-building template.
