For Kimberley businesses

You can do the work. We help with the rest.

Pricing jobs. Writing tenders. Chasing variations. Making sure you get paid on time. It's a full-time job, and most Kimberley businesses can't justify hiring someone full time to do it. So don't.

Fractional Commercial Manager — the short version: you don't need a full-time commercial manager. You need one some of the time. That's what a fractional commercial manager is — you get the person, you just share them.

Multi-Trade Commercial Coordination — the short version: ready for bigger, multi-trade packages? We handle the subcontractor agreements, payment claims and principal reporting so you can safely head contract, without carrying a full-time commercial team.

Sound familiar?

Most businesses up here run into the same wall. It isn't about whether you can do the job.

  • You know the work is worth more than you charged, but the price was already in
  • You did extra work on site and never got paid for it, because nobody wrote it down at the time
  • You've been waiting 60 or 90 days for money you're owed
  • You didn't make the shortlist, and nobody told you why
  • The tender was 80 pages and due Friday, and you had a crew to run
  • Someone sent you a payment claim and you weren't sure what you had to do, or by when

None of that is a sign you're bad at your trade. It's a sign you're missing a commercial manager. Bigger companies have one. They're expensive — a full-time salary plus a vehicle and everything else — and a business the size of most Kimberley operators can't carry that.

Did you know?

If you don't reply to a payment claim in time, you can end up owing the full amount.

If someone sends you a payment claim and you don't reply properly in time, you can end up owing the whole lot — even if you disagree with it.

The rules under WA's security of payment laws are strict, and the clock doesn't stop for site work, holidays or a busy week. Most businesses that get caught out weren't disputing the claim. They just missed the reply.

It works the other way too. If you're owed money, those same laws are the fastest way to get it — but only if your claim is put in properly.

Getting this right is part of what a commercial manager does.

What it costs you

Most of this gets paid for by the job, not by you.

This is the part people don't expect. Once you win the work, the cost of a commercial manager sits in the job's price — the same way your site supervisor or your insurance does. The head contractor funds it through the rates.

Stage 1

Tender support

We help you put the tender together. Fixed fee, capped hours, agreed before we start — so you know the number up front.

You pay this one out of pocket. If you don't win, that's where it stops — there's no further fee.
What normally happens Stage 2

Ongoing commercial management

You win the job, and we stay on to run the commercial side of it. Monthly fee, minimum term.

Costed into the tender price as a preliminaries item. Funded by the head contractor through your rates.
Stage 3

Buy-out

If you win the job and would rather run the commercial side yourself, you can. A one-off buy-out fee applies.

Also costed into the tender price. This is what you pay to end the arrangement, not an alternative to it.

To be clear about Stage 1: that fee is at your risk. If the tender isn't successful there's no job to cost it into, so it stays out of pocket. It's kept deliberately small for that reason — it's there to make sure we're both serious, not to make money.

We don't publish our fees, because the right number depends on the size and length of the job. Ask and we'll give you a figure before you commit to anything. Travel outside Broome is charged at what it costs us, nothing on top. GST applies.
