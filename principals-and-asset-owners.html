Principals & Asset Owners

“Who can see what a contractor is claiming?”

Superintendent’s Representative and contract administration from someone who has won tenders, reviewed contracts and built claims — so the claim in front of you is read by someone who knows how it was assembled.

The asymmetry

A contractor’s claim is prepared by people who do this every month. It arrives with a schedule, a set of variations, a delay narrative and a number. On the other side of it is often a project manager, an asset team or a council officer with a dozen other responsibilities and no commercial background — reading a document designed by someone who knows exactly which parts will not be tested.

That asymmetry is not bad faith. It is simply what happens when one side has a commercial function and the other does not. It shows up in predictable ways:

  • Progress claims certified against the previous claim rather than against the work actually in place
  • Variations conceded on presentation, because entitlement and pricing were never separated
  • Extension of time claims granted or refused without the programme analysis to support either answer
  • A payment schedule that misses its statutory deadline, and an amount that becomes payable whether or not anyone agreed with it
  • Acquittal reporting to a funding body that cannot be traced back to the certified claims underneath it

The cost of each of those is real, and none of them are recoverable once the certificate is issued. The point of an independent commercial read is to catch them while they are still decisions rather than history.

What this looks like in practice

An independent read on what is being claimed.

Claims

Assessed on their merits

Progress claims read against the contract and the work actually in place, not against the last claim. Where quantities or rates do not reconcile, the query goes back with the reasoning attached, so the position is defensible if it is later disputed.

Variations

Tested before they land

Entitlement and pricing examined separately. Whether the contract creates an entitlement at all is a different question from what the work is worth, and running them together is how variations get conceded on presentation. Alternate pricing options put up where the scope allows one.

Time

Statutory clocks held

Security of payment timeframes do not stop for site work, a busy week or a holiday period, and they are generally not extendable. Payment schedules and responses go out inside time, which is the single cheapest piece of commercial protection available to a principal.

Reporting

That survives scrutiny

Acquittal and progress reporting a probity officer, an auditor or a funding body can follow from the certified claim through to the reported figure. Grant-funded work is where this matters most, because the reporting obligation outlives the project.

Contract

Read before it is signed

Contract and tender documents reviewed for the provisions that decide how the job runs — valuation of variations, time bars, notice requirements, latent conditions and the security regime. Cheaper to fix in the draft than to argue about in month eight.

Market

Priced against regional reality

Tendered rates tested against what Kimberley work actually costs — mobilisation, seasonality, haulage and availability. A price that is too low is a risk to a principal, not a saving, because the recovery attempt arrives as variations.

The appointment

Superintendent’s Representative.

Nausicaa offers Superintendent’s Representative and contract administration as a service. Under the standard Australian contract forms the role carries a duty to act honestly and fairly in valuing and certifying, and that duty runs to both parties — it is not a principal-side advocacy position, and it should not be sold as one.

What a principal gets from it is not a harder line. It is a defensible one: a certificate that was arrived at by someone who has priced this kind of work, built these kinds of claims and knows what the contract actually requires before a valuation is made. The liability that attaches to certification is the reason the appointment is priced above contractor-side administration.

Engagement is not conditional on the appointment. Plenty of this work is a contract administration or claim-review engagement without a formal Superintendent’s Representative appointment. If all you need is a second read on a claim before you certify it, that is a fixed-fee piece of work, not a retainer.
