Delivering in the Kimberley · Service

Local content and participation planning.

WAIPS and APP participation plans built on a supplier base that has actually been verified — and reported on weekly through delivery, not reconstructed at close-out.

Scope of service

  • WAIPS Industry Participation Plans — preparation, submission, variation and close-out reporting
  • Aboriginal Procurement Policy (APP) plans — target-setting against verified capability, not supplier registers
  • Social Procurement Framework — compliance assessment and response
  • On-ground engagement with Traditional Owner representatives — heritage, access and cultural considerations identified early, in person
  • Engagement with Ranger groups — land management, environmental works and local operating knowledge
  • Engagement with local Indigenous businesses — capability and capacity confirmed directly, and subcontract structures that let them participate without carrying risk they cannot manage
  • Weekly operational and commercial reporting — participation performance tracked against commitments every week, so gaps surface while they can still be fixed
  • Supplier identification and pre-qualification — Kimberley and Pilbara, verified first-hand
  • Subcontractor panel establishment — documentation, invitation and award support

The part most plans miss

Weekly reporting. A participation plan that is only reported at close-out is a plan nobody can correct. Weekly tracking is what turns a commitment into a result — and what produces the evidence trail when performance is questioned.

Why this is a compliance risk, not a policy goal

  • WA Government contracts above threshold carry mandatory WAIPS commitments — contractual obligations with reporting requirements, not aspirations
  • Larger contracts trigger APP obligations alongside the Social Procurement Framework
  • Failure to meet commitments at close-out carries commercial and reputational consequences, and follows the contractor into the next evaluation
  • Plans returned for revision cost programme time that a tender submission date does not have

Why remote-written plans fail

  • Suppliers nominated who do not have the capacity to perform at the level promised
  • Targets set against a supplier base nobody has verified
  • Trade categories chosen where no Kimberley capability exists — steel fabrication, specialist electrical and instrumentation, most licensed trades at scale
  • The gap between what was promised and what is available then gets managed for the life of the project

What the Kimberley supplier market actually holds

The Kimberley does not have the supplier depth of the Pilbara. Setting targets that survive close-out scrutiny means knowing the difference between what is on a supplier register and what a business can realistically perform.

Genuine depth

  • Labour hire and site services
  • Civil plant operations and earthworks
  • Logistics and transport
  • Camp catering and facilities
  • Security and site access
  • Land management and environmental works

Thin or absent

  • Complex structural steel fabrication
  • Specialist electrical and instrumentation
  • Most licensed trades at scale
  • High-volume specialist plant

What a credible participation plan contains

  • Description of the works, package by package
  • Identification of WA industry capability in each trade package
  • A methodology for engaging WA suppliers — not a statement of intent
  • Measurable commitments with defined reporting milestones
  • A mechanism for addressing gaps where targets are not met

Plans that do not address each element are returned for revision.

Aboriginal participation

  • APP compliance requires more than directing work to an Aboriginal-owned business
  • The obligation extends to structuring the engagement so the business can actually perform
  • That means confirming capacity, setting appropriate subcontract terms, and supporting capability gaps that would otherwise prevent delivery
  • It requires a conversation that cannot be had from a Perth office

Conflict management

Local content planning is buy-side work — advising the contractor or principal on how to meet obligations. Capability coaching for Aboriginal and regional businesses is sell-side work. Nausicaa will not act for more than one party on the same project where those parties are in a contractual relationship with one another, and does not accept commissions or referral fees from any business it recommends. Where a business recommended in a deliverable is a current or former retained client, that is disclosed in the deliverable itself.
