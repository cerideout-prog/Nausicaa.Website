Executive Summary

Prominent Hill is a remote underground copper-gold operation in South Australia, then owned by OZ Minerals and expanding through the ~A$600 million Wira Shaft development. Micromine was engaged to deploy Pitram — a mine-production and fleet-management system — to give the operation material tracking for grade control through its ore passes and to measure the productivity and effectiveness of a growing underground fleet, running over a newly installed private underground network. As Micromine's Senior Mining Solutions Manager (Global) and commercial lead for Pitram in APAC, Callum owned the engagement end to end: offsite scoping and requirements analysis conducted remotely under COVID travel restrictions, the commercial negotiations, and implementation across roughly 50 pieces of heavy mobile equipment over an 18-month programme. Midway through, two things changed at once — OZ Minerals was acquired by BHP, requiring the relationship, key stakeholders and contractual terms to be re-established; and Micromine moved to a full subscription commercial model that materially increased the ongoing cost of the software. Callum managed both, keeping the client and the commercial position intact and making Prominent Hill one of Micromine Pitram's most significant and complex Australian engagements.

The Challenge

Prominent Hill is a long-life copper-gold mine in a genuinely remote part of South Australia, roughly 130 kilometres south-east of Coober Pedy. As the operation transitioned deeper and expanded — the Wira Shaft underground expansion was approved in 2021 to extend mine life toward 2036 — it needed far better visibility of two things: where material was going and at what grade as it moved through a series of ore passes, and how productively and effectively its heavy mobile fleet was working. Pitram was the system chosen to provide that, deployed onto machines and run over a newly installed private underground communications network.

Four things made the engagement demanding. First, complexity: this was a combined hardware-and-software deployment across roughly 50 pieces of heavy mobile equipment in a live, expanding underground environment, not a desktop software rollout. Second, remote scoping: the initial scoping and requirements analysis had to be conducted offsite because of COVID-era travel restrictions, without the usual ability to walk the site. Third, a change of owner mid-project: OZ Minerals was acquired by BHP, which meant re-establishing the relationship, the key stakeholders and the contractual terms with a new and much larger counterparty partway through delivery. Fourth, a change of commercial model: during the same period Micromine moved Pitram to a full subscription model, significantly increasing the ongoing cost of the software — a difficult conversation to hold with any client, let alone one going through its own ownership transition.

Callum's Role

Callum led the engagement for Micromine — the technology provider — as Senior Mining Solutions Manager (Global) and the commercial lead for Pitram across the APAC region. He owned both the commercial relationship and the delivery of the system.

His responsibilities ran from first contact to steady-state operation: scoping and requirements analysis; the commercial negotiation and structuring of the agreement; oversight of the implementation across the fleet; and ownership of the client relationship through two significant disruptions — the acquisition of OZ Minerals by BHP, and Micromine's transition to a subscription pricing model. In practical terms, he was the person accountable for the engagement delivering for both the client and the company: the technology landing and being used, and the commercial arrangement remaining sound.

Approach

Remote scoping and requirements analysis

With COVID travel restrictions preventing site access, Callum ran the scoping and requirements analysis offsite — defining what the operation needed Pitram to do for grade control and fleet productivity, and how the system would map onto Prominent Hill's ore-pass material flow and equipment, without the benefit of being on the ground.

Commercial negotiation and structuring

He led the commercial negotiations and structured the agreement for a substantial, multi-year engagement — balancing what the client needed against a sound commercial position for Micromine.

Implementation across the fleet and network

He oversaw the deployment of Pitram — both the in-cab hardware and the software — across roughly 50 pieces of heavy mobile equipment, leveraging the newly installed private underground network so that material tracking for grade control and equipment-productivity data flowed from the machines back to the control room in a live operating mine.

Holding the relationship through an ownership change

When OZ Minerals was acquired by BHP, Callum re-established the relationship and the contractual terms with the new owner and worked through a materially changed set of key stakeholders, keeping the engagement on track rather than allowing the transition to stall it.

Managing a commercial-model change with stakeholders

In parallel, he managed the shift to Micromine's full subscription model — a significant step-up in the ongoing cost of the software — with stakeholders on both sides, so that the change was absorbed without losing the client or the commercial value of the engagement.

Why It Matters

This profile shows an executive who can hold a complex, high-value engagement together across both its technical and commercial dimensions — and, more tellingly, through two simultaneous disruptions that were largely outside his control. A corporate takeover changed the counterparty mid-delivery; his own company changed its pricing model at the same time. Either can derail a project. Managing both at once, in a remote underground operating environment, while still landing a hardware-and-software deployment across a fleet of machines, demonstrates the intersection of commercial judgement, delivery discipline and stakeholder management that separates an account manager from a genuine commercial operator. It is directly relevant to any organisation that needs someone to protect a customer relationship and a commercial position when the ground shifts beneath a project.
