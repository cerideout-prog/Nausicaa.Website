Project Profile — Mining & Civil Services / Organisational Scaling

Building the Corporate Structure to Hold Three-Fold Growth

A 21-month general management tenure that took a Kimberley field-maintenance business into contract mining, civil construction and transport — and built the functions, systems and commercial discipline to make that growth hold.

Executive Summary

Kaynar Group is a Broome-based mining, civil and maintenance contractor. In December 2024 it was a field-based maintenance labour business of roughly 46 people, run directly by its founders. Within three months of Callum joining it had won a waste mining package at Kimberley Mineral Sands' Thunderbird operation and scaled to around 155 people — growth arriving faster than the business had structure to absorb. Callum was brought in as General Manager specifically to build that structure: reporting lines and KPIs, commercial ownership, policy and HSEQ systems for disciplines Kaynar had never delivered, and standalone HR, recruitment, commercial and marketing functions where none existed. He left in September 2026 with the business operating from a purpose-built Broome facility, running civil and transport divisions alongside its maintenance core, and holding multi-year contracts priced and negotiated under his commercial authority.

The Challenge

When Callum joined, Kaynar was a business built on one thing done well: supplying skilled tradespeople to remote mine sites. Its corporate infrastructure was minimal and entirely appropriate to that size — an office manager handling rostering, a director running payroll, and the Managing Director making most operating decisions directly. There was no HR function, no finance function separate from the family, no commercial function, and no defined reporting structure.

Then the growth arrived. A New Year's Eve 2024 bid for the waste mining package at Thunderbird was won, and the business scaled from roughly 46 people to around 155 within three months, hiring close to 100 operators and mobilising equipment from more than six suppliers. At the same time Kaynar took on a tailings storage facility construction contract another contractor had left behind, entered civil construction, stood up a Main Roads-registered transport company, and moved into production mining — four disciplines it had not previously delivered.

The risk was structural rather than commercial. A business can win work faster than it can build the systems to price it, staff it, govern it and get paid for it. Rapid headcount growth without a corporate spine produces the failures that end contractors: unpriced risk in tenders, inconsistent pay and rostering, uncontrolled hiring, no HSEQ system for work outside the original scope, and no reliable view of project profitability.

Callum's Role

Callum was General Manager, reporting to the Managing Directors, Kyle Ringin and Shaylee Greechan.

The role carried an unusually heavy commercial weight for a general management position. Callum held tender authority and hiring decisions, and owned profit forecasting, commercial modelling, and the commercial and account relationships across the client base.

His direct reports were the Operations Manager, the Recruitment and Talent Lead, the commercial function, site supervisors and external consultants. The Workshop and Asset Manager reported to him with a dotted line to the Directors. He led and managed the HR function without being its direct line manager — a deliberate separation, keeping employee relations independent of the executive making hiring and commercial decisions. Finance reported directly to the Directors as an internal family function.

Approach

Company structure, reporting lines and KPIs

Callum designed and implemented the organisational structure the business had not previously needed: defined divisions, reporting lines, and KPIs against which functions and projects could be measured. He then led a RACI process across teams and projects to settle who was accountable for what — the necessary follow-on once a structure exists on paper.

Building functions from nothing

Every corporate function Kaynar now runs was built during this period. HR, recruitment and talent, commercial and marketing did not exist as functions in December 2024; the work was absorbed by an office manager, a director and the Managing Director. Callum built each as a discipline with its own ownership. HR and finance, previously handled internally within the family, now operate as standalone functions with dedicated hires.

Commercial ownership and contract renewal

With tender authority, Callum owned pricing and commercial structure across the contract base. Three engagements show the scope: Kaynar's appointment as the Shire of Broome's tier-one plant and machinery maintenance provider on a three-year term; a three-year extension of the road maintenance contract at Thunderbird, where he led the contract review, pricing, commercial model and contract format; and the extension of the Ellendale engagement from a remote workshop into the supply of operators and equipment alongside the existing mobile workshops.

Policy, procedure and HSEQ for new disciplines

Moving into mining, civil and transport meant operating under obligations Kaynar's maintenance-focused systems did not cover. Callum led and implemented the operational policy suite, safe work procedures, competency assessment documents and project HSEQ functions the new work required.

Local workforce and training pathways

The Kimberley labour market is small and heavily contested, and most major clients in the region carry local employment requirements. Rather than treat local content as a compliance line, Callum built the recruitment and talent function around it, and worked alongside Managing Director Shaylee Greechan with North West Regional TAFE on training pathways based in the region. Kaynar is led by an Indigenous co-founder — Shaylee Greechan, of Baard and Yawuru heritage — and its Broome workshop team is Indigenous-led under the Operations Manager, who reported to Callum.

Pay bands, systems and data discipline

He developed a pay band calculator built on skills, experience and port of home, replacing case-by-case negotiated pay with a defensible, data-driven framework. In a labour market where the business competes with major resource projects for the same people, consistency in pay is both a retention tool and a cost control. On the finance side his role was to advise rather than own: the migration from MYOB to Xero and the establishment of general ledger cost tracking against projects were driven within the finance function, with Callum consulted on both.

Outcomes

  • ✓Workforce scaled, then right-sized. From 46 people to around 155 at peak, settling at 80 permanent full-time employees — scaling up for a fixed-term mining contract and back down to a sustainable base without a disorderly demobilisation.
  • ✓Three new capabilities established. A civil construction division, a Main Roads-registered transport company, and roughly six months of production mining delivered at Thunderbird.
  • ✓Multi-year contracts secured under his commercial authority. Shire of Broome tier-one plant and machinery maintenance (three years); Thunderbird road maintenance extension (three years); Ellendale extended into operators and equipment.
  • ✓Corporate functions now standing on their own. HR, recruitment, commercial and marketing established as functions; HR and finance moved out of internal family administration into standalone teams with dedicated hires.
  • ✓Equipment availability sustained above 90%. Availability is the product Kaynar sells — it is what keeps client projects running — so holding it above 90% through a period of tripled headcount and four new disciplines is a direct measure of whether the operating systems held.
  • ✓Local and Indigenous employment held through the scale-up. Kaynar's workforce at Thunderbird ran above 50% Kimberley-local and above 35% Indigenous during the period of fastest hiring — the point at which local content is hardest to hold — and the apprentice intake grew over the tenure.
  • ✓A permanent home. The business relocated to a purpose-built workshop and headquarters on its own 13,000 m² site in Broome.
  • ✓External recognition of the period. At the 2025 Broome Business Excellence Awards, Kaynar Group won the Inclusion and Workplace award and Managing Director Kyle Ringin was named Business Person of the Year. In May 2026, Kyle Ringin was named First Amongst Equals at the Business News 40under40 awards and also won the Family Business category. These are the company's and its founder's achievements; Callum was General Manager throughout the period they recognise.

Why It Matters

Growth is the easy thing to point at; whether a business survives it is a different question. This profile shows an executive hired specifically to build the structure that makes fast growth durable — organisational design, commercial control, governance systems and functional capability — in a business that had none of it and needed all of it at once.

The commercial weight of the role is the distinguishing feature. Tender authority, pricing, profit forecasting and account ownership sat with the same person accountable for building the operating structure, which is why the two stayed connected rather than drifting apart. And the more telling outcomes are not the peak headcount: they are that the workforce came back down to a sustainable base without disorder, and that equipment availability held above 90% throughout.
