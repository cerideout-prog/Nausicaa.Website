The problem with remote administration

Most contractors delivering Kimberley work are based in Perth, Adelaide or interstate, and so are their commercial and contract administration functions. That creates a structural exposure: every payment claim, every variation assessment, every extension of time response and every subcontractor notice runs on a clock, and the clock runs regardless of where the administrator is sitting.

Statutory and contractual timeframes in this sector are strict and generally not extendable. The compressed notice periods in AS4000 and AS2124 compound the risk — variations require contemporaneous assessment or the contractual entitlement is prejudiced, and the contemporaneous record is what determines whether a claim succeeds two years later.

On-ground contract administration is not a convenience. It is a risk management decision, and it is priced as one.

Two distinct roles

Before engaging, identify which role the project requires. They carry different obligations, different liability profiles and different fee structures, and they are not interchangeable.

Higher liability · Higher fee

Superintendent's Representative

Appointed under the contract — typically AS4000 or AS2124. Acts honestly and impartially in certification and valuation.

  • Appointed under the head contract, not employed by either party
  • Duty to act honestly and impartially in certifying claims, valuing variations and assessing extensions of time
  • Will not accept direction as to the outcome of any certification, valuation or determination
  • The principal pays; the impartiality duty is unaffected by that
  • Higher professional indemnity exposure, reflected in the fee
  • Appropriate for principals and government clients
Standard liability · Standard fee

Contractor-side contract administration

Acts solely as the contractor's agent. No impartiality duty — your interests only.

  • Agent of the contractor, with no duty owed to the other party
  • Prepares and submits payment claims within time
  • Assesses, prices and substantiates variations in the contractor's interest
  • Administers the subcontract suite on your behalf
  • Maintains the contemporaneous record that supports later claims
  • Appropriate for head contractors and subcontractors

Security of payment

The Building and Construction Industry (Security of Payment) Act 2021 (WA) governs payment claims and payment schedules on construction contracts in this State. Its provisions apply to contracts entered into on or after the relevant commencement date, with the earlier Construction Contracts Act 2004 (WA) continuing to apply to contracts entered into before it.

The mechanism that matters commercially is straightforward. A respondent who receives a payment claim must reply with a payment schedule within the period fixed by the Act. A respondent who does not becomes liable for the full amount claimed, as a statutory debt, regardless of the merit of the claim. There is no extension mechanism and no discretion to cure it after the fact.

The exposure, stated plainly

The amount at risk on a missed payment schedule is the amount claimed, not the amount in dispute. On a project with monthly claims across several subcontractors, that exposure recurs every month for the life of the contract — and every one of those windows has to be covered by someone who is actually available to cover it.

Statutory position stated as at August 2026 and subject to verification against the current legislation. This is a description of a commercial risk, not legal advice. Engagement models

Contract administration is priced as a monthly fee across the programme, or against contract value — not as a day rate. The incentive alignment matters: a day-rate administrator benefits from complexity, a fixed monthly administrator benefits from resolution. Superintendent's Representative appointments are priced higher, reflecting the impartiality duty and the liability that attaches to certification. Multiple concurrent projects are supported.

Discrete assignments — supervision, expert review, short-form work — are available at $1,280 per day, based on $160 per hour over an eight-hour day. Client travel is recovered as a disbursement at cost.

Conflict management

Where Nausicaa holds a Superintendent's Representative appointment, it will not concurrently act for any retained client that holds, or is tendering for, a subcontract under that head contract. Nausicaa will not act for more than one party on the same project where those parties are in a contractual relationship with one another, and will not hold any commercial or margin interest in works it administers or certifies. Nausicaa accepts no commission, referral fee or rebate from any party it recommends or assesses. A conflict check is performed and recorded before every engagement.
